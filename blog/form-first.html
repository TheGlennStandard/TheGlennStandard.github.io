Form First: Why I Stop Sets Early

By D'Vante Glenn, CSCS · September 2026 · 2 min read

Last week I stopped a client three reps into a set of eight. The weight was not heavy. He was not in danger. "That was fine," he said.

Fine is the problem.

His third rep was slower than his first. His hips drifted and his brace went soft. His body found a way to move the bar with less work from the muscles we were there to train. Five more reps would have been practice at a mistake.

Every rep is a vote for the movement you will own. They all count, especially the sloppy ones.

Why it matters in every sport

Form is not about looking right. It does three jobs.

It is where results live. When form breaks, the load moves to your lower back, shoulders or knees. Clean reps keep the work where you want it.

It protects you. Most people get hurt on a pattern, not on the bar. One small compensation, repeated ten thousand times. A pitcher with a sloppy landing. A volleyball player who lands stiff. A golfer who swings from the lower back.

It trains attention. My athletes at Roosevelt hear this daily. How you do the warm up is how you will do the game.

Slower is faster

A client who spends four weeks on a clean hinge owns every deadlift, swing and jump after that. An athlete who learns to land before we chase jump height is still playing in the fall.

If you train with me
