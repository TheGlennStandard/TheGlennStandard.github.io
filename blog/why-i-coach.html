Why I Coach

By D'Vante Glenn, CSCS · September 2026 · 3 min read

People expect a big moment: a championship, an injury, a speech. It was quieter. Hundreds of small moments when someone decided I was worth their time.

I grew up in Chicago. The city gives you toughness you did not ask for and, if you are lucky, an adult who sees something in you first. For me those adults were coaches. They showed up on cold April mornings and taught me that how you do the small things is how you do everything.

Baseball took me to Coe College. We won two regular season conference titles and a regional championship. I studied kinesiology, and what my coaches taught by instinct started making sense on paper. Why the drills worked. Why rest mattered. Why the athletes who moved well stayed healthy.

I found my path through baseball. My own development inspired me to share what I know and give others the same confidence and results I gained through a consistent strength and conditioning program.

Learning by coaching

After Coe I coached at Olive-Harvey College, where the program went from 5 wins to 28 and five players made all region. The number I like best is not on a stat sheet: players who learned to show up early and train with intention.

Wins are rented. Standards are owned. I want people to know what their own best effort feels like, so they can repeat it.

Every body deserves a coach

Today I coach strength at Roosevelt University. I support 17 NCAA Division II teams and directly oversee baseball, softball, and men's and women's volleyball. Away from campus I train adults in Chicago and run online programs for athletes and clients anywhere.

The 20 year old shortstop, the volleyball hitter and the 58 year old executive need the same things. Someone who watches every rep, tells the truth with respect, and builds a plan around their real life.

That is why I coach. People invested in me, and that debt gets paid forward. If you wonder whether you are the kind of person who works with a coach, you are. Come find out.
