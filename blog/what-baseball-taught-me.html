What Sport Taught Me About Training for Life

By D'Vante Glenn, CSCS · September 2026 · 3 min read

Baseball is a strange game to love. It is slow when you want it fast. It humiliates you in public. The best hitters ever failed two of every three tries.

I played at Coe College, then in the Wisconsin State League with the Kenosha Kings and the Northwoods League with the Wausau Woodchucks. I trained and played with the Black Sox, a group of amateur and pro players working to move up. Then I coached. I was head coach of the Panthers in 2022, and at Olive-Harvey we went from 5 wins to 28. Today I coach strength for 17 college teams. Every sport I work with runs on the same lessons.

1. You cannot cram

The swing you bring to opening day is the swing you built in November in a cold cage. A volleyball player's jump in October is built in June. People who look strong in August trained in February.

When a new client asks how fast results come, I say: faster than you fear, slower than you hope, and permanent if we do it right.

2. Failure is data

Strike out, walk back, make one adjustment, believe in the next at bat. Every sport teaches it.

A missed lift is not a verdict. It is information about what to do next.

Adults quit programs over one bad week. Athletes cannot, there is a game tomorrow. Bad session, short memory, next pitch.

3. Preparation is respect

The coaches who shaped me treated preparation as character. You take pregame grounders like it is the ninth inning. My clients get a plan written for them. My athletes get a warm up they can explain. If I expect your best hour, you get mine.

4. Nobody develops alone

Every athlete is a group project: parents, coaches, teammates. Most adults train alone with earbuds in, until they stop. A coach knows your swing, notices when it is off, and cares if you show up Thursday.

5. Play the long season

You cannot win a season in April, but you can lose it there, by getting hurt or burning out early. Your health is the longest season there is. Show up, protect your body, stack small wins.

I still love this game. What I love most is what sport builds in people, and I get to pass it on.

See you at the rack. Coach D
