Longevity

Train for Decades, Not the Summer

By D'Vante Glenn, CSCS · September 2026 · 3 min read

The fitness industry has sold the same picture for fifty years: the beach body, the eight week shred. It sells because it is fast and visible.

I care about a different picture. A person in their seventies getting up off the floor without using their hands. Carrying groceries up the stairs. Catching themselves on a curb instead of falling. Playing catch with a grandkid, then again tomorrow because nothing hurts.

That picture is built in the weight room, decades ahead.

Strength is the account nobody opens

From our thirties on, we lose muscle and strength, and bone density follows. Power, the ability to produce force fast, falls fastest. It is what saves you on an icy sidewalk.

None of it is destiny. Training bends every one of those curves at any age. It protects bone, keeps muscle and steadies blood sugar. Most of all it protects your independence. The gap between a bruise and a broken hip is often strength built years earlier.

Every rep in your thirties and forties is a deposit your seventy year old self gets to withdraw.

Same plan, different ages

I use the same principles for a 20 year old shortstop, a college volleyball player and a 60 year old executive. Move well. Get strong. Stay consistent. Respect recovery.

The athlete trains for this season and for the body she lives in after sport. The executive trains for energy this quarter and for travel at 80. A golfer who keeps rotational power at 60 keeps playing at 70.

Never too early, never too late

Young athletes. Training now builds movement quality and tough tissue for life. I will not trade that for a quick number on a radar gun.

Adults who think the window closed. It did not. People who start strength training in their fifties and sixties gain real strength, bone and function. The best time to start was 20 years ago. The second best is this week.

Health is the budget. Everything you love gets paid out of it. Train for the body you will live in. It is the only one you get.

Coach D'Vante Glenn, CSCS
