The Habits That Outlast Motivation

By D'Vante Glenn, CSCS · September 2026 · 2 min read

Every January, and most Mondays, I meet people running on motivation. They bought the shoes and saved the plan. They are fired up.

Motivation leaves in two or three weeks. It does that for everyone, including me and the college athletes I coach. The people who change are not more motivated. They built a week where training happens anyway.

Programs end. Motivation fades. Habits compound.

Four habits I coach

A standing appointment. Tuesday 6:30. Thursday 6:30. Saturday 9. Decide once, when you are at your best. Athletes do the same: lift days are set before the schedule fills up.

A minimum day. Travel, deadlines, sick kids. Everyone needs a 15 minute session they can do anywhere with no equipment. A person who trained for 15 minutes is still a person who trains. Players on the road with a club team use it every week.

Sleep on the calendar. A perfect program loses to five hours of sleep. Set a bedtime the way you set a squat day.

A two minute review. After each session, two questions. What got better? What gets attention next time?

Identity beats willpower

The people who last are not gritting their teeth. Training is part of who they are, like being a parent or a Chicagoan. You build that by keeping small promises until the evidence piles up.

Where a coach fits

A coach is a discipline strategy. Nobody expects an athlete to develop alone. A coach holds the schedule, watches your form and remembers your goals on the days you forget them.

Spend your motivation on setting up the system, not on surviving workouts. When it leaves, the system stays.
