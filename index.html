How I Coach

Three things I promise every client

01

Form First

Technique before load, always. A rep done right is worth ten done fast. I teach you the why behind every cue, so the quality stays even when I'm not watching.

02

Habits Over Hype

Programs end. Habits compound. We build sleep, movement, and recovery routines that will still be paying you back in twenty years.

03

Championship Standard

I prepare D-II athletes for conference titles and residents for life. Different goals — the exact same care, preparation, and standard.

Why Coach D

Most trainers have a certificate.
This is a different résumé.

Anyone can call themselves a trainer. Very few hold the credential college and pro teams require, coach NCAA athletes daily, won championships in uniform — and deliver it all with five-star service.

Educated & certified at the highest level

A kinesiology degree plus the CSCS — the NSCA credential required by college and professional teams, held by a small minority of trainers. It demands a bachelor's degree and a rigorous exam most never attempt.

Translation: the person watching your squat studied the science of it.

Proven where it's hardest

Two-time conference champion as a college ballplayer. As a coach: a program built from 5 wins to a school-record 28, two conference championships, and a summer club that produced seven All-Stars and a league batting champion. Today: strength coach to 17 NCAA Division II programs.

The Coach

From the South Side to the strength room

I grew up in Chicago and found my direction on a baseball field. Playing college ball taught me what preparation really means; studying kinesiology taught me why it works; coaching — from community college dugouts to an NCAA weight room — taught me how to bring it out of other people.

Today I coach Division II athletes at Roosevelt University and private clients at NEMA Chicago. Whoever you are, you get the same thing: my full attention and my highest standard.
