Why this is different

Most trainers guess at baseball.
I've lived every seat in the dugout.

Player, college assistant coach, summer-league head coach, and now NCAA strength & conditioning coach across 17 programs. Your hitting instructor sharpens the skill — I build the engine underneath it: rotational power, sprint speed, a durable arm, and a body that lasts into August.

The engine, not the swing

Exit velocity and arm speed come from the ground up — hips, trunk, legs. We develop force production and rotational power so every lesson your skills coach gives you actually shows up in games.

Durability is a skill

The best ability is availability. Arm-care routines, landing mechanics, and smart workloads — because the players who develop are the ones still on the field in the playoffs.

Age-appropriate, always

A 13-year-old and a college junior should never train the same way. No maxing kids out, no radar-gun shortcuts — development sequenced to the athlete's body and training age. Parents welcome at every session.

The Training Year

A plan for every season

Off-Season

Build (Oct – Jan)

The money months. Strength foundations, rotational power, sprint development — the biggest physical gains of the year happen when nobody's watching.

Pre-Season

Sharpen (Feb – Mar)

Convert strength to speed. Throwing workloads ramp, power work peaks, and we taper so you arrive at tryouts fast, fresh, and confident.

In-Season

Sustain (Apr – Sep)

Short, precise sessions that maintain strength without stealing game-day legs. Most athletes get weaker across a season — mine don't.

For Parents

Straight answers

What age should my ballplayer start strength training?

Properly coached, strength training is safe and beneficial from early adolescence — the research is clear on this. What matters is who's coaching it: age-appropriate loading, movement quality first, and no ego lifting. That's exactly what "form first" means.

Will lifting make my kid slow or "muscle-bound"?

The opposite. Speed comes from force applied to the ground; a stronger athlete who moves well is a faster athlete. Every program I write pairs strength with sprint and movement work.

Can you help with recruiting?

I've sat in the college dugout and coached players into four-year programs. I'll give you an honest read on where your athlete stands, what actually moves the needle for recruiters, and how to be smart about the process — no false promises.

Do you coordinate with hitting/pitching instructors?

Yes, gladly. Skills coaches and I are on the same team: they refine the movement, I build the athlete underneath it. I'll also coordinate throwing workloads so nobody's arm pays the price of poor communication.
